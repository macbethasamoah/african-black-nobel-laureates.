II. Slow-Look Carbon Historiography

"In an era saturated by fleeting digital images, hyper-realism demands an act of deliberate stillness. There is a profound silence when raw carbon meets woven cotton. By stripping away the distraction of color, we confront the unadorned topography of wisdom—every crease of endurance and luminous gaze etched into archival permanence."

Material Standards

Technical & Archival Specifications

Constructed to museum conservation standards, each piece in the 35-work cycle is engineered for multi-generational longevity, optical depth, and structural stability across diverse climatic environments.

  • Substrate & Support Heavyweight, acid-free triple-primed archival cotton canvas stretched over kiln-dried hardwood bars (40cm × 40cm uniform square format).
  • Pigment & Medium Graduated museum-grade graphite (4H through 9B) layered with compressed willow and carbon charcoal for velvet shadow density.
  • Conservation & Fixative Sealed with multi-layer archival UV-resistant matte museum fixative to eliminate graphite glare and prevent surface oxidation.

Artist Biography & Practice

Asamoah Twum Macbeth

Hyper-Realistic Portraiture & Visual Historiography • Accra, Ghana

Working from his studio in Accra, Ghana, Asamoah Twum Macbeth is a contemporary visual artist dedicated to hyper-realistic portraiture and historical documentation. Through disciplined mastery of archival graphite and charcoal on canvas, his practice transforms monochrome draftsmanship into a vessel for Pan-African and global Black memory.

Viewing every human face as a biographical landscape, Macbeth approaches portraiture as an act of archival preservation. His landmark multi-year cycle, The African & Black Excellence Collection, unites 36 African and Black Nobel Laureates across continental Africa and the diaspora—creating a permanent monument to shared intellectual and humanitarian sovereignty.
